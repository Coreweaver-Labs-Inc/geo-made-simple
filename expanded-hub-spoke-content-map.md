# Expanded B2B Growth Topics Map

**Status:** Editorial architecture only. This map does not authorize publication of a research finding, client result, comparative claim, performance figure, testimonial, or case study.

## Purpose and Scope

The existing `/topics` directory remains Coreweaver Labs’ public **B2B Growth Topics** hub. Its five established pillars—B2B SEO, Content Marketing, Paid Ads, AI Representation, and Content Governance—are sound buyer-facing categories. The next expansion should deepen those categories around the work a mid-market B2B team must actually coordinate: explaining an offer, teaching a buying group, connecting paid and organic messages, making public information easier to interpret, and keeping claims current.

The map therefore expands **depth before breadth**. It avoids generic trend commentary, “ultimate guides,” and pages created solely to capture search traffic. Google’s guidance favors content with a clear intended audience, original value, transparent authorship, and useful internal links rather than scaled, search-engine-first production. [1] [2] Demand-generation material may connect awareness, education, sales context, and measurement, but Coreweaver should present these as operating choices rather than promise pipeline or revenue outcomes. [3]

> **Editorial standard:** Every proposed page must help a mid-market B2B operator make one clearer decision, inspect one system, or improve one handoff. If it cannot, it remains an internal signal note rather than a public page.

## Article Brief Standard

Each planned page must be briefed before drafting. The brief establishes the intended reader value and prevents pages from becoming near-duplicates or generic keyword summaries.

| Brief field | Required decision |
|---|---|
| **Primary buyer question** | One plain-language question that the page will answer. |
| **Reader and moment** | Role, decision context, and what they need to do next. |
| **Distinct contribution** | The method, checklist, framework, or original analysis that makes the page useful beyond a summary. |
| **Source plan** | Tier 1, 2, or 3 sources; publication date; method visibility; and named owner. |
| **Claim ledger** | Exact claim, supporting source, scope, limitation, reviewer, and publish/revise/remove decision. |
| **Internal links in** | Parent hub plus at least one contextual page or approved Insight. |
| **Internal links out** | Parent hub, one adjacent topic, one method/evidence page, and the relevant service connection. |
| **Boundary statement** | A topic-specific no-guarantee or no-unverified-proof statement. |
| **Refresh trigger** | Source change, product/service change, material reader confusion, or scheduled evidence review. |

## Internal-Linking Architecture

The map preserves the existing rule that each Insight links to one primary topic pillar. It adds a clear depth rule: an Insight may also link to **one** deeper briefing page when that page helps the reader make the next decision. The page should never become a link list.

| From | To | Required context |
|---|---|---|
| `/topics` | Each established pillar and, once approved, the cross-cluster coordination page | A one-sentence buyer problem, not a keyword label alone |
| Existing pillar page | Its three to five child pages | An explicit explanation of the child decision it helps solve |
| Child page | Parent pillar, one adjacent child page, one method/evidence page, and `/services` when relevant | Descriptive anchor text embedded in a useful sentence |
| New Insight or field brief | One parent pillar and at most one child page | The reader’s next useful question, not a promotional handoff |
| `/services` | The three launch-service pillars plus one coordination explanation | Service relevance, not promised results |
| `/research`, `/faq`, `/framework` | Only topics directly clarified by their method, objection, or operating-system context | Do not force all hubs to link to every child page |

Google advises that each page worth finding should receive at least one relevant, crawlable link, and that anchors should be concise, descriptive, and surrounded by useful context. [2] The map therefore assigns each child page a minimum of two inbound links before it is treated as established: its parent pillar and one contextually related public page.

## Governance Lanes

Not every asset belongs in the same publishing lane. The lane determines whether a topic can publish as an evergreen explainer or requires the stronger research workflow.

| Lane | Eligible assets | Required review | Not allowed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Evergreen explainer** | Definitions, checklists, decision frameworks, blank worksheets, and methodology explanations | Author, source check, claim review, internal-link review | Unsourced market figures, client outcomes, named comparisons, or implied guarantees |
| **Field brief** | Source-backed analysis of a timely operating question | At least two relevant sources, author, claim ledger, reviewer | Trend aggregation without a contribution or unsupported ‘best practice’ claims |
| **Original research brief** | Audited dataset, authorized survey, disclosed method, or cross-functional analysis | Method, source notes, limitations, research/editorial/privacy review as applicable | Pre-written conclusions or client evidence without authorization |
| **Authorized proof** | Case study or verified client evidence | Existing seven-field intake, reviewer handoff, written publication authorization | Synthetic examples presented as client work, testimonials, or results |

## First 90-Day Sequence

The sequence is deliberately modest. It creates linkable depth around the launch services while allowing review effort to remain concentrated.

| Period | Publishing candidate | Gate before drafting | Post-publication action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–2 | Website information architecture; buyer enablement | Source plan and brief approved | Add parent and Framework links; log reader questions |
| Weeks 3–4 | Paid-message/landing alignment; claim ledger | Boundary review completed | Add service, FAQ, and governance links |
| Weeks 5–6 | AI answer review; content refresh | AI/disclosure and source review completed | Link to AI Data Policy and Research methods |
| Weeks 7–8 | Buyer-language audit; campaign learning loop | Worksheet and measurement limits reviewed | Link to relevant existing Insights |
| Weeks 9–10 | Content review workflow; source-of-truth pages | Cross-functional owner review completed | Check two inbound links per child page |
| Weeks 11–12 | Expert-led thought leadership; growth-system coordination | Two supporting public pages and source plan exist | Decide whether to publish, revise, or retain as internal brief |

This is a review cadence, not a traffic forecast. Search discovery, audience response, citations, rankings, and commercial outcomes remain independent variables.

## Validation Checklist

Before any new route is added, verify the following:

1. The page has a precise buyer question, unique contribution, named author, source plan, and topic-specific boundary.
2. Every material factual statement has a claim-ledger entry with scope and limitation.
3. The page links through crawlable HTML anchors to its parent and at least one relevant existing page.
4. The page has no duplicate purpose, generic keyword stuffing, fabricated proof, or unsupported business outcome claim.
5. A reviewer confirms that no confidential, client-identifying, or unauthorized evidence is present.
6. The title, description, canonical, structured data, SSR output, and sitemap behavior are reviewed before release.
