# Coreweaver Labs — Manual Authority and Clutch-Readiness Scorecard

## Purpose

This scorecard supports a **quarterly manual review** of controllable evidence quality and independent market signals. It does not rank the company, predict a Clutch outcome, or convert activity into a commercial-performance claim. No schedule, external integration, or automated data collection is enabled until the owner approves the required systems and definitions.

## Quarterly Review Rules

The scorecard owner records the reporting window, source location, calculation, known limitations, and reviewer for every reported metric. A blank value means “not collected” rather than zero. Trend comparisons are allowed only when the definition and source stayed consistent.

| Review principle | Required behavior |
|---|---|
| Separate owned from independent signals | Do not present publishing output as external authority |
| Preserve the denominator | Record the number of eligible records as well as completed records |
| Capture limits | Note source changes, missing data, small samples, and attribution uncertainty |
| Link to evidence | Save URLs, dashboard exports, approved internal reports, or decision records privately |
| End with a decision | Continue, revise, pause, archive, or investigate—never “optimize” without a stated reason |

## Quarterly Review Sequence

1. Close the reporting window and freeze source exports or source URLs.
2. Have each metric owner enter the value, denominator, source, and limitation.
3. Review evidence quality before discussing direction of change.
4. Separate changes Coreweaver Labs controlled from third-party decisions, client sentiment, and market conditions.
5. Publish no external claim from the scorecard unless it has a separate approved source and wording review.
6. Record the next-quarter decision and the responsible owner.

## Clutch Readiness Interpretation

Clutch identifies reviews, clients and experience, market presence, and specialization as relevant to its provider assessment. The scorecard therefore tracks evidence that can improve readiness, not a promise of a directory position.[1] The owner should not treat sponsored placement, where available, as an organic-rank result; it is a separate paid-distribution decision documented independently.
