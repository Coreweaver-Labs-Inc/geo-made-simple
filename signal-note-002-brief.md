## Approved Sources

1. [Google Search Central — Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content). The note uses the current guidance that content should offer clear sourcing, evidence of expertise, accurate authorship context where expected, and a people-first purpose. It also uses the page’s warning against exaggerated titles and content created primarily for search-engine visits.
2. [Google Search Central Blog — Understanding the sources behind Google News](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2021/06/google-news-sources). The note uses Google’s explanation that clear dates, bylines, author information, and organizational context can help readers understand an article and its creator. This is used as a reader-context principle, not as a claim about Google News eligibility or ranking.

## Coreweaver Contribution

The Signal Note translates the sources into one bounded operating decision: **a public B2B statement should carry its current source, scope, limitation, owner, and review point before it is repeated elsewhere**. The contribution is an editorial practice for the already-published B2B claim ledger—not a generic transparency summary or a claim about audience trust, compliance, or search results.

## Claim Boundary and Limitation

The source documents support people-first content, clear sourcing, authorship context, and reader-facing transparency principles. They do **not** establish that a claim ledger, qualifier, source link, byline, or review routine will create trust, prevent legal or compliance issues, improve rankings, produce AI citations, generate leads, or cause commercial results. The Signal Note makes no such claim. It is an editorial operating interpretation, not original research, a compliance standard, or a performance study.
