## Source Contract

| Source-supported principle | Use in the guide | Prohibited inference |
|---|---|---|
| Google’s AI features surface relevant links and may use different techniques and models. | Explain why a review begins with a specific buyer question and visible source context rather than a universal “AI score.” | That Coreweaver can control a platform response or that the same result will appear for every person, model, query, date, or locale. |
| Google says a page must be indexed and eligible for a snippet to be eligible as a supporting link in its AI features; there are no additional technical requirements. | Point readers to crawlability, indexability, textual availability, helpful content, internal links, and matching visible structured data. | That technical readiness guarantees AI feature appearance, a link, or a citation. |
| Google’s guidance favors useful, non-commodity, people-first content and warns against scaled page creation or special GEO “hacks.” | Explain why each new source-of-truth page must solve a distinct reader decision rather than target every answer variation. | That a certain word count, “chunking,” llms.txt, special schema, or keyword expansion earns AI visibility. |
| Google encourages clear authorship and context about how content is created where a reader would reasonably expect it. | Explain why a review record identifies an owner, source, limitation, and next review action. | That attribution by itself creates authority or a ranking outcome. |

## Draft Shape

1. **Buyer question:** Frame the decision as “What should we inspect?” rather than “How do we get cited?”
2. **Review path:** Identify question, context, current source of truth, ambiguity, owner, and next action.
3. **Public-information boundary:** Explain the difference between an observable answer context and a claim about model behavior.
4. **Related resources:** Route readers to AI policy, claim ledger, research standards, Method, and information architecture.
5. **Blank worksheet:** Use browser-local prompts only; forbid customer data, raw transcripts, credentials, and unreviewed prompt logs.
6. **Boundary statement:** Close with no guarantee of appearance, description, citation, retrieval, traffic, or commercial outcome.
